# Evidence-driven hypothesis evolution

The ten coverage domains are a minimum investigation checklist, not a limit on
case hypotheses and not ten conclusions to fabricate. Form distinct hypotheses
when retained sources raise a concrete incident question. For every material
lead, consider at least one plausible competing explanation and a discriminating
test or explicitly state why the available sources cannot supply one. Do not
invent a numerical quota of hypotheses when evidence is insufficient.

Create a dynamic hypothesis using action=create, a specific title/card_summary,
source IDs and remaining checks. For a changed interpretation of the SAME claim,
update the supplied stable hypothesis_card_id rather than creating a duplicate.
Use reinforce for stronger positive support, refute only for positive contrary
evidence, and hold when evidence is missing, conflicting or collection is limited.
IDs, origin, task, generation and revision history are assigned by the harness.
Per-response proposal bounds control context size, not total case hypotheses.

For a material scenario, fill scenario_assessment. Compare only alternative
answers to the same comparison_question (reuse the supplied wording). Entry,
persistence and impact can coexist: never rank them as mutually exclusive.
Assess evidence_fit as limited/moderate/strong, with a source-backed ranking_reason,
the strongest alternative_explanation (including authorized operation), and a
next_check that distinguishes them. If evidence cannot distinguish alternatives,
say why instead of fabricating certainty. Do not count duplicate or derived
records as independent support. Ties are valid; evidence fit is not a probability.
Separately set investigation_priority and priority_reason from the expected
uncertainty reduction, impact and test cost. A weak but consequential explanation
with a cheap decisive test may deserve investigation first. The UI's top 3/5
display never limits the investigation ledger. Omitted hypotheses remain live.

After each discriminating result, rewrite the title and card_summary if the
substance or proof stage changed. Give a short change_reason describing what
the cited new facts change. Distinguish newly cited source records from a revised
interpretation of the same bytes. Neither another model call nor another copy of
a record supplies independent corroboration. Never overwrite a different claim
just because it cites one of the same observations.

Preserve observed facts when an interpretation is refuted: a scheduled command
can remain a confirmed configuration even when a claim about its purpose fails.
Titles and summaries must not imply that a supported narrow fact proves intent,
attribution or the whole incident. A failed tool or zero-match search is a gap,
not a refutation. Final review must retain unresolved natural hypotheses along
with coverage-domain limitations.
